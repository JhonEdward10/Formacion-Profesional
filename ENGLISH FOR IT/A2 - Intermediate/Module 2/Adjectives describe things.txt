Adjectives describe things and feelings, and they change form to compare or show extremes:

Regular Adjectives:

Add "er" for comparatives (e.g., "safer") and "est" for superlatives (e.g., "safest").
Use "more" and "most" for adjectives with two or more syllables (e.g., "more secure," "most secure").

Irregular Adjectives: Change in unique ways:

Good → better, best
Bad → worse, worst
Far → farther/further, farthest/furthest
Little → less, least
Much/Many → more, most

Special Cases:
Adjectives ending in "y" change to "ier" and "iest" (e.g., "happy," "happier," "happiest").
Use "than" with comparatives and "the" with superlatives (e.g., "faster than," "the fastest").

Understanding regular and irregular adjectives lets you compare effectively and make your sentences richer. Keep practicing to learn them.
